WHAT HAPPENS AT THE BORDER

Borders judge a shipment by its paperwork.

01

Customs

Customs checks what is entering the country: what the goods are, where they come from, and whether duties and rules apply.

02

Health authorities

Health and plant or animal health authorities check that the goods are safe and properly certified for the country they are entering.

03

The documents

Officers work from the paperwork. Certificates, lab reports, invoices and declarations must agree with each other and with the rules.

Critical Risk: If the paperwork does not match, authorities can hold or reject the cargo.

WHAT VERIPURA CHECKS

Every document the route requires.

Shipping and trade

Bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, purchase order. Certificates of origin and preferential origin claims. Fumigation certificates. FDA Prior Notice. Customs code and tariff advice.

Health and safety

Health and phytosanitary certificates. Certificates of analysis and lab reports. Establishment and orchard registrations. VeriPura checks safety scheme certificates, such as FSSC 22000, for expiry.

Buyer-required certifications

Halal, organic, kosher, cruelty-free and vegan. VeriPura checks the certifier against the official list, including whether its recognition has lapsed, and looks it up on national registers where they exist.

Border documents

Organic certificates. Deforestation declarations with plot maps. Seafood catch certificates. Wine certificates. Composite products.

Labels and artwork

VeriPura validates labels against the rules of the destination: additives, allergens, banned colorants. Halal artwork. Vague “green” claims with no proof.

Across every document

VeriPura cross-checks weights, dates, names and certificate numbers, spots forged or altered documents, screens companies against sanctions lists and for forced labour, chases missing papers and raises expiry alerts.
